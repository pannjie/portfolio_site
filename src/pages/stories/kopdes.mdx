Can a chain of state-run 7-Elevens revive Southeast Asia’s largest economy? Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto certainly seems to think so. His latest megaproject is the Red-White cooperatives (KOPDES), a chain of 83,363 ‘minimarts’ that offer both cheap groceries and low-cost loans to villagers in need.

In theory, these cooperatives will revive the country’s ailing economy by cutting out those pesky ‘profiteering’ middlemen. In practice, the flagship [US$23.8bn](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/04/26/analysis-unclear-financial-framework-of-red-and-white-village-cooperatives) project has become an endless source of internet memes.

One viral social media video shows a [cooperative built atop Mount Prau](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Bq2HlTC8B4), 1385m above sea level and hours away from any prospective customers. Another image shows [several cooperatives floating](https://youtu.be/HeOpqM6ngjY?si=JzZdjXyl66NLfMU3&t=72) sedately in the Java Sea.

Officials laughed off the nautical outlets as a minor mapping error, but it’s hard to joke about the [deaths of five co-op managers](https://en.tempo.co/read/2110676/5-trainees-die-during-military-training-for-indonesias-cooperative-program), who passed away during the programme’s mandatory military training. Why these managers needed weapons training before their ‘deployment’ continues to be a mystery.

The financial danger is no laughing matter either. Of the 83,363 cooperatives set up on paper, 96% of them recorded no transactions in 2026. As of October, 87% of all cooperatives still had no members on their books. It is unclear how many have finished construction and how many are still blueprints.

However, even the fully operational co-ops offer cause for worry. Nganjuk regency’s 238 coops in East Java were inaugurated in May 2026 by Prabowo himself and they are the highest grossing in the entire nation. They recorded transactions totalling just US$850,899 since May, against an estimated budget of [US$35-40 million in capital](https://en.antaranews.com/news/390453/each-village-cooperative-costs-rp25-billion-to-build-minister) and about US$300,000 in salary costs. Neither the actual operating costs nor the profits are publicly available.

It’s no surprise that critics are calling for more transparency, if not a total pause on the programme. Many have highlighted its risks to Indonesia’s fragile economy. The KOPDES programme is partially funded through village funds, which are used as collateral to secure loans from state-owned banks. Repayment is funded by the profits from the cooperatives.

Should the profits fail to materialize, the cost of debt repayments could cripple not just village finances, but the entire state banking sector. Research by the [CELIOS](https://celios.co.id/red-and-white-cooperatives-or-red-and-white-squeeze/), a Jakarta-based think tank, shows that 8.5% of loans provided to all cooperatives are ‘non-performing loans’, a percentage much higher than the national average. By forcing banks to lend to these unproven businesses against their own financial judgment, the administration risks destabilizing the wider financial system, which is already under strain from [an MSCI downgrade](https://thediplomat.com/2026/10/indonesian-stock-exchange-plunges-after-fresh-msci-downgrade-threat/).

KOPDES is the latest megaproject in a nation that has seen many such ideas come and go. Prabowo’s MBG (Free Nutritious Meals) programme was scaled back after corruption and food safety concerns. His predecessor Jokowi’s new capital of Nusantara sits in limbo due to the lack of political capital.

Whether the coops share their fate depends on the government’s willingness to adjust course should things go awry. There have been accusations of [corruption in the procurement process](https://www.detik.com/bali/hukum-dan-kriminal/d-8574767/pengadaan-pikap-kopdes-merah-putih-diduga-di-mark-up-hingga-rp-69-juta-unit). The military has [gotten involved in supplying medicines to the coops](https://tirto.id/tni-bakal-produksi-obat-dukung-gerai-apotek-kopdes-merah-putih-hbaY). Experts are also concerned that these coops will compete with—and cannibalize—existing businesses.

President Prabowo is characteristically defiant. In speeches, he has mocked his critics. These naysayers, he claimed, are backseat drivers more concerned with [the choice of wood](https://en.tempo.co/read/2097058/prabowo-uses-bridge-building-analogy-to-slam-uncooperative-critics) than the bridge being built before their eyes. Knowing his reputation for magical thinking on fiscal matters, investors and neighbours are quietly sceptical.
